## Day 2: The line on a haul-out quote that costs the most
Photo: travel-lift.jpg

Hi,

Last time I sent you the haul-out checklist. Here is the one thing I would add to it.

The line on a yard quote that costs owners the most is rarely a big number. It is "as required".

"Antifoul: as required." "Anodes: as required." "Sundries: as required."

Each one is an open cheque. You will not know what it cost until the invoice arrives, and by then she is back in the water.

The fix is free. Ask for every line to say three things: what it is, how much of it, and the price. Paint brand and litres. Anode sizes. Hardstand days.

Try this week: pull out last year's quote and highlight every "as required". That is your list of questions for the yard.

In a few days I will send you a real example of what a haul-out check turned up.

## Day 5: A worked example (honest illustration, NOT a real client)
Photo: shoot-engine-room.jpg
Subject: What a haul-out check can turn up: a worked example

Hi,

Here is a worked example of what a haul-out check can turn up. It is an illustration built from the kinds of things we look for. It is not a particular yacht or client.

**Picture this:** a 55 ft flybridge motor yacht with around 2,400 engine hours, coming out of the water in spring.
**What you check first:** the sea-trial readings before she lifts, then the running gear and the through-hulls on the hardstand.
**What a check like this can find:** a shaft-seal drip rate higher than last year's log, and sacrificial anodes worn almost to nothing, on a quote that says "anodes: as required".
**Why it matters:** worn anodes stop protecting the metal around them, so the next thing to wear is the part you did not want to replace.
**What good looks like:** the anodes renewed with the sizes written on the quote, and the seal serviced while she is out of the water.
**The takeaway:** compare this year's readings with last year's, and get anode sizes on the quote, not "as required".

If you would like the checks we use, they are in the checklist from last week.

## Day 9: How to read a yard quote in five minutes
Photo: running-gear.jpg

Hi,

A yard quote is easier to read once you know what a good one looks like. Five minutes, six questions:

1. Does it include the lift both ways, and how many hardstand days?
2. Does it name the paint: brand, grade and litres?
3. Is every extra a priced line, not "as required"?
4. Who inspects what, and will you get photographs?
5. Does it name the anodes, with sizes?
6. Is the relaunch date booked?

If you can answer yes to all six, the quote is in good shape. If not, you know exactly what to ask before you sign.
