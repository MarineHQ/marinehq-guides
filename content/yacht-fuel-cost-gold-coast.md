A 70 to 80 ft luxury motor yacht burns about **50 to 70 litres an hour at 10 to 12 knots, and 330 to 490 litres an hour at 28 knots**. At the October 2026 diesel price of $3.24 a litre, that is **$162 to $221 an hour slow and $1,056 to $1,575 an hour fast**. Speed is the biggest thing you control, and this guide shows what it costs, boat by boat, using published figures for Azimut, Pershing and Sunseeker.

- **Pershing GTX80** — about 68 L/h at 10 knots, 439 L/h at 28 knots

Fuel is the cost that moves with how much you use the boat. The berth, insurance and registration arrive on the same dates whether she leaves the pen or not. Fuel doesn't, and the price per litre has moved sharply this spring. If you are budgeting a year, our guide to [the cost of owning a yacht on the Gold Coast](/guides/cost-of-owning-a-yacht-gold-coast/) puts fuel in context with every other line.

## What diesel costs today

No Gold Coast marina we found publishes its pump price. The nearest published price is the Royal Queensland Yacht Squadron's, in Brisbane, updated on 1 October 2026:

| Fuel | Members | Non-members |
|---|---|---|
| Premium diesel | $3.19 a litre | $3.24 a litre |
| Premium unleaded | $3.00 a litre | $3.05 a litre |

A month earlier, on 3 September, the same club listed diesel at $2.79 to $2.84 a litre. That is about 14% more in four weeks. Every dollar figure below uses **$3.24 a litre**, the non-member price, so it is the cautious end. Ask your own fuel dock for today's price, and date any number you rely on.

### Two Sunseekers

Independent sea-trial tables for a Sunseeker of 74 to 76 ft are hard to find. The best published figures are the builder's own numbers, as listed by YachtBuyer. They are **not** independent sea trials, and the page does not say what load, weather or sea state they were measured in. We have used them because they are what is published; treat them as a guide to the size of the bill.

| Yacht | Engines | Speed | Burn | Cost an hour | Cost a nautical mile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sunseeker 74 Sport Yacht (74 ft) | Twin MAN V12, 1,550 hp | 12 knots | 50 L/h | $162 | $14 |
| | | 28 knots | 486 L/h | $1,575 | $56 |
| | | 34 knots (top) | 565 L/h | $1,831 | $54 |
| Sunseeker 76 Yacht (76 ft) | Twin MAN V12, 1,550 hp | 23 knots | 290 L/h | $940 | $41 |
| | | 31 knots (top) | 600 L/h | $1,944 | $63 |

The 76 Yacht's listing gives a 10 knot range of 500 nautical miles but no burn figure at that speed, so there is no slow-speed row for it. Above about 80 ft we found no published burn figure we could trust; ask the builder for the fuel curve for your engines.

Three things stand out.

- **Slow is a different cost entirely.** The Pershing at 10 knots costs $221 an hour. At 28 knots it costs $1,423, over six times as much, to cover 2.8 times the distance.
- **The last few knots cost the most.** The Azimut S7 burns 140 more litres an hour going from 28.9 to 36.3 knots, and the Sunseeker 74 costs $256 an hour more at 34 knots than at 28.
- **Your boat will not match these.** Engines, load, weather, sea state and hull condition all move every number.

<figure><img src="/assets/photos/shoot-at-helm.jpg" alt="A skipper seated at the helm of a motor yacht" loading="lazy"><figcaption><b>The throttle is the biggest fuel decision.</b> On the Pershing GTX80 test, 10 knots cost $221 an hour and 28 knots cost $1,423.</figcaption></figure>

A hundred hours at 28 knots is not a hundred hours at the same distance. At 28 knots the Pershing covers about 2,800 nautical miles in 100 hours; at 10 knots it covers 1,000. Fuel is the cost of the hour, which is why the cost per mile in the tables above is the fairer comparison.

<div class="callout orange" markdown="1"><span class="eyebrow">Budget trap</span>
Most owners quote a fuel figure from a brochure or a broker. Ask which speed it was measured at. The number at 10 knots and the number at 28 knots can differ by more than a factor of six.</div>

## How to work out your own number

You need three things: the litres an hour at the speed you will run, the hours underway, and the price a litre.

1. **Litres an hour.** Ask the builder or your engineer for the fuel curve for your engines, or look up a published sea trial for your model.
2. **Hours underway.** Count only the engine hours, not the time at the dock.
3. **Price a litre.** Use today's price from your fuel dock, and add a margin because the price has moved this spring.

For example, a Pershing GTX80 on a two-hour run at 28 knots: 439 litres an hour × 2 hours × $3.24 = **$2,845**. The same two hours at 10 knots cover about a third of the distance and cost 68 × 2 × $3.24 = **$441**.

Two things the tables do not include. The generator burns fuel at anchor and overnight, and a speed-and-burn table says nothing about a night at a mooring. And a hull with growth on it burns more at the same speed; our [antifouling guide](/guides/antifouling-gold-coast-cost/) covers how often the Gold Coast's warm water makes that necessary.

## Where fuel money goes

- **Speed, not distance.** The Pershing's cost per mile at 28 knots is more than double its cost at 10 knots, and the Sunseeker 74's is about four times.
- **Price timing.** Diesel rose about 14% between September and October 2026. A trip priced a month ago is not the trip you pay for now.
- **Filling from empty.** A full tank is a five-figure cheque on a boat this size. Pay attention to when you fill, not only how much.
- **A fouled hull.** A hull with growth burns more fuel at the same speed, so part of the antifoul pays for itself.
- **Generator and hotel loads.** Air conditioning and a generator running at a weekend anchorage add litres the speed tables do not show.
