A 60 ft motor yacht kept on the Gold Coast costs roughly **$70,000 to $80,000 a year sitting at the dock**, and **$120,000 or more once you use her** for a hundred hours. Bigger boats, Sanctuary Cove berthing, agreed-value insurance or a big engine year push that higher. Here is where the money goes, using published 2026 rates wherever one exists.

Most owners find the fixed costs are predictable and the variable ones are not. The berth, the insurance and the rego arrive on schedule. The yard visit, the engine service that turns into an injector job, and the fuel bill after a good month on the water are what move the total.

A few things the rate card does not shout about:

- **Membership matters at Southport.** The visitor rate is 40% to 50% above the Gold Member rate. A 12-month contract on a 59 ft single berth is $19,311 for members.
- **Power is extra.** Southport bills three-phase electricity at 35 cents a kilowatt hour. GCCM lists utilities as extra too.
- **Wide and catamaran berths cost 30% to 40% more** at Southport; GCCM charges multihulls 1.5 times on a T-head and double in a berth.
- **Rates are climbing.** Southport's 59 ft double berth went from $14,139 a year in May 2023 to $16,087 in May 2026, up 13.8% in three years.
- **New marinas open at the top of the market.** Mantaray Marina at The Spit, opened May 2026, advertises monthly leases from $2,000 and twenty superyacht berths.
- **Buying is an option.** Freehold berths at Hope Island, next to Sanctuary Cove, list at about $179,000 for 12 m and $320,000 for 18 m, with outgoings of $6,600 to $7,900 a year. Rentals in the same area run $1,050 to $1,300 a month.

## The yard: antifouling and everything that goes with it

Warm water fouls hulls fast, and the Gold Coast is named in industry guides as a high-fouling area. Plan on antifouling every 12 to 18 months, or every 12 to 15 months for an aluminium hull.

GCCM's all-inclusive antifoul package is the clearest published price on the Coast: **from $89.50 a foot**, including haul-out and relaunch, waterblast, environmental levy, four days on the hardstand, International Micron One or similar, a running-gear check and yard management. It applies to fibreglass monohulls **up to 50 ft** that were antifouled within the last two years. A 50-footer is therefore about $4,475. Anything larger is a personalised quote. The same package was $69 a foot when it launched in 2020, so it has risen about 30% in six years.

For the antifoul work alone, HF Marine Services at GCCM publishes from $51 a foot for a standard two-coat job and from $86 a foot with a hull cut and polish, excluding the yard's lift and hardstand, Propspeed, anodes and any fairing.

## Insurance

No Australian insurer publishes a rate as a percentage of hull value. The nearest independent figure is that boat insurance in Australia typically costs **1% to 3% of the insured value a year**. On a $1.2 million motor yacht that is $12,000 to $36,000. Where you sit in that range depends on the boat's age, your experience and claims history, where she is kept and whether you insure for agreed or market value.

Queensland has one extra variable: cyclones. Brisbane, the Gold Coast and the Sunshine Coast attract smaller cyclone loadings than the Whitsundays or Cairns, but most policies still require you to haul out or secure the vessel within a set time once a named cyclone is within a defined distance. Nautilus, for example, applies a named-cyclone excess of $5,000 or 5% of the claim, whichever is greater. Read that clause before you buy, not after.

## Servicing the machinery

Engine makers set service intervals on **hours or calendar time, whichever comes first**. For most leisure boats that means an annual service whether you did 40 hours or 400. Volvo Penta's D-series has a 250-hour or yearly service (filters, belts, seawater pump, anodes) and a 500-hour or two-yearly service on top; IPS pods add gear oil, drive anodes and diagnostics, and a five-yearly pod service out of the water. Generators run on a similar pattern, with oil at 150 hours or yearly on an Onan and an annual impeller, filter and anode change on a Kohler.

Australian guides put a routine inboard engine service at **$1,000 to $5,000 per engine**, and unplanned repairs at a further $1,000 to $5,000 a year. Those figures skew small; a twin-engine 60-footer with a generator should allow for the upper end every year and for a much larger bill in a 1,000-hour year, when injectors, aftercoolers and heat exchangers come due. No Australian dealer publishes fixed prices for a Volvo IPS, MAN, MTU or Caterpillar major, so get it quoted early. Seatech Marine at The Boat Works is the authorised Kohler, Onan and Caterpillar dealer on the Coast.

## Registration

Queensland registration is one of the cheaper lines. The fee is set in the Transport Operations (Marine Safety) Regulation and indexed each July.

| Vessel length | 2025–26 | From 1 July 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Over 10 m to 15 m (33 to 49 ft) | $608.05 | $628.60 |
| Over 15 m to 20 m (49 to 66 ft) | $753.60 | $779.05 |
| Over 20 m (66 ft and up) | $948.25 | $980.25 |

Vessels of 15 m and over have one extra step: any new registration, transfer or change of particulars must be endorsed by Maritime Safety Queensland before a Transport and Main Roads service centre will process it.

## Fuel: the cost of actually using her

Marina diesel in south-east Queensland was **$2.79 to $2.84 a litre** at the Royal Queensland Yacht Squadron on 3 September 2026, the nearest published pump price to the Gold Coast. Southport Yacht Club gives Gold Members 6 cents a litre off at its 24-hour fuel wharf, and Sanctuary Cove offers bulk pricing on fills over 1,000 litres. Prices moved sharply in late August 2026, so date any figure you rely on.

What a motor yacht burns at cruise, from factory and press sea trials:

At 180 litres an hour and $2.84 a litre, a 55 to 60 ft yacht costs **about $510 an hour underway** at fast cruise. A hundred hours a year is $51,000, and the generator, running at anchor and overnight, is on top. Drop to displacement speed and the same M55 burns 33 litres an hour; the difference between 10 knots and 23 knots is roughly $420 an hour.

## Keeping her clean

Every Gold Coast detailer quotes on request; none publishes a price list. The nearest published Australian rates are a Sydney yard's: a wash at $4.75 to $5.00 a foot for 41 to 60 ft boats, wax from $3 a foot and a cut and polish at $18 to $20 a foot. On that basis a 60-footer is about $300 a wash and roughly **$7,800 a year fortnightly**. A full cut and polish is a four-figure job and usually timed with the yard visit.

## Where the money hides

- **The gap between "at the dock" and "in use" is fuel.** Decide how much you will really use her before you decide what she costs.
- **The yard year.** Antifoul is annual-ish, but a pod service, a shaft seal or an aftercooler can double the bill in the year it falls due.
- **Member versus visitor.** At Southport the difference on a 59 ft single berth is about $13,000 a year.
- **Cyclone clauses.** Cheaper cover with a haul-out requirement is not cheaper if the yard has no space when a cyclone is named.
- **Everything is quote-only above 50 ft.** The only way to know is to ask, and to ask for the full scope.
