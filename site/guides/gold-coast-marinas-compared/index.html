Gold Coast marinas compared: where to keep a 40–90 ft motor yacht

Sanctuary Cove, Southport Yacht Club, The Boat Works, GCCM, Hope Harbour, Marina Mirage, Runaway Bay and more, compared on berths, depth, fuel, haul-out, distance to the Seaway and published rates.

Updated 6 September 2026·11 min read·Gold Coast, Queensland

The Gold Coast has eleven commercial marinas and about 170 superyacht berths, more than anywhere else in Australia, so the question is rarely whether there is a berth. It is which one. The honest split is Broadwater marinas at Main Beach and Runaway Bay, five minutes from the Seaway and priced accordingly, versus Coomera River marinas an hour and a half upstream at six knots, next to the shipyards and out of the weather.

Key facts

  • Closest to open water — Marina Mirage and Southport Yacht Club (about 5 minutes to the Seaway); Runaway Bay (2 nautical miles)
  • Closest to the trades — The Boat Works and Gold Coast City Marina, in the Coomera precinct with 300-tonne lifts on site
  • Only published rate card — Southport Yacht Club, effective 1 May 2026; everyone else quotes on enquiry
  • Speed limit — 6 knots from Paradise Point all the way up the Coomera River
  • Channel depth — about 3.0 m at Sanctuary Cove and GCCM; 3.5 m at The Boat Works at low tide
  • New in 2026 — Mantaray Marina at The Spit (opened August); Main Beach Superyacht Marina under construction

Sanctuary Cove Marina

The resort marina, and the address most people picture. Sanctuary Cove is a Platinum Gold Anchor, Superyacht Ready marina and was Marina of the Year in 2023. It takes vessels to 65 m with a 3.0 m draught and 15 m beam, and its J Pier expansion added ten 30 m and fifteen 18 m berths, bringing it to 63 superyacht-capable berths. The fuel jetty runs 24/7 on card swipe with fast-flow diesel at 120 litres a minute and bulk pricing on fills over 1,000 litres. Fob access to berths and amenities is 24 hours, with CCTV and patrols, three-phase power, laundry, private showers, Wi-Fi and a helipad.

What it does not have is a yard. Sanctuary Cove markets its proximity to the Coomera precinct for refit work, which in practice means a short run upriver to The Boat Works or GCCM for every haul-out. It also does not publish rates or a live-aboard policy; berthing is by enquiry for casual, permanent and superyacht categories.

Southport Yacht Club Marina, Main Beach

The only Gold Coast marina with a public rate card, and the one to benchmark everything else against. Three hundred berths, vessels to 130 m, power to 600 amps, an official Customs clearance port and Australia’s largest floating superyacht berth at 160 m. Five minutes from the Seaway, with a 24-hour self-serve fuel wharf and a hardstand with a 35 to 40 tonne travel lift for boats up to 55 ft. On site: the Waterfront Restaurant, the Bosun’s Locker chandlery, a members’ gym, laundry and pump-out.

There is a waiting list for permanent berths, reported at around 300 vessels, and the club is adding 19 berths.

The Boat Works, Coomera

A working marina inside a 55-acre shipyard, and the biggest lifting capacity in the city: a 300-tonne Marine Travelift, a 100-tonne and a 70-tonne lift, plus a 45-tonne hydraulic lift for wide catamarans. The South Marina has 125 work berths, with around 150 in-water service berths and 300 hardstand allocations across the site, and 15 superyacht berths to 50 m with a 75 m alongside berth. Water is 3.5 m at low tide, maintained by dredging. It is about 11 nautical miles from the Seaway.

The Boat Works is where you go to get things done: 80 to 90 marine trade tenants, owner-operated work allowed at no extra charge, a bunkering dock, pump-out, 24/7 CCTV with on-site caretakers, live-aboard ensuites, a hardstand area for up to 16 live-aboard boats, courtesy cars, an owners’ lounge and three places to eat. It earned 5 Gold Anchors and Superyacht Ready status in 2023, and during Cyclone Alfred in March 2025 it secured about 400 vessels with no damage. Rates are not published.

Gold Coast City Marina & Shipyard (GCCM), Coomera

Marine HQ’s home base, and the other half of the Coomera precinct. GCCM, operated by d’Albora, has 200 berths for vessels of 10 to 75 m, on-water service berths to 75 m, 24-hour security with site-wide CCTV, three-phase power to 125 amps and a 24/7 tap-and-go fuel wharf with bunkering on request. The shipyard runs 50-tonne and 300-tonne lifts, 50,000 m² of hardstand, sheds from 8 to 45 m, 300 dry-stack spaces and more than 90 businesses. It was the first shipyard in the Asia-Pacific to receive 5 Gold Anchors and took Marina of the Year, Boatyard of the Year and Dry Stack Facility of the Year at the 2025 industry awards.

GCCM’s last published berth schedule is from November 2022, marked as subject to change and excluding utilities: 51–60 ft $1,650 a month, 61–70 ft $1,925, 71–80 ft $2,400, 81–90 ft $3,200. Multihulls pay 1.5 times on a T-head and double in a berth. Freehold berths also change hands here; a 16 m berth on Pier H listed with annual outgoings of $4,245.

Hope Harbour Marina, Hope Island

The first marina upstream after Paradise Point and, at its own estimate, 15 minutes from the Seaway. Its 280 berths take vessels from 6 to 40 m including multihulls, on composite pontoons in a basin branched off the river, which keeps it out of the tidal flow and is why it is marketed as cyclone and flood shelter. Fuel jetty with premium unleaded and diesel, 24/7 CCTV with on-site guards, chandlery, café, laundry, tender service, boat ramp and three-phase power on the larger berths, with the Ramada hotel, Marlin bar, pool and tennis courts alongside. No yard. Rates are by application.

Marina Mirage, Main Beach

Seventy-six to seventy-eight deep-water berths from 12 m to 70 m and beyond, 3.5 to 4 m at the berth, five minutes from the Seaway and next door to Southport Yacht Club’s fuel wharf, since Mirage has none of its own. Power from 15 to 250 amps, a helipad, 24-hour security with key-fob gates, undercover parking and the La Luna floating beach club. The rebuilt marina reopened in 2021 and took Marina of the Year (under 140 boats) in 2023.

The big news is the $500 million Makris redevelopment: the retail precinct closed in November 2025, a 126-room Luxury Collection resort and 38 residences are on the way for 2029, and the marina is to keep operating through construction. Rates not published.

Runaway Bay Marina

Two nautical miles from the Seaway with 195 wet berths for 8 to 30 m vessels and 260 dry-stack spaces to 10 m, a fuel dock, a 35-tonne travel lift for boats to 16 m, and a good cluster of trades on site including Short Marine and Gold Coast Antifouling. Security is 24/7 CCTV with on-site guards. Staying aboard for a night or two is allowed at no charge; full-time live-aboard is not offered. Quote form only for rates.

Pacific Point Marina (formerly Horizon Shores), Steiglitz

Now a d’Albora marina and renamed. More than 550 wet berths for vessels to 40 m, 330 dry-storage spaces, a 70-tonne travel lift, refit sheds, 24/7 fuel, a chandlery seven days a week and after-hours patrols. It sits on southern Moreton Bay near Jacobs Well rather than on the Broadwater, which suits owners heading north to Moreton Bay more than those running out the Seaway. Live-aboard is permitted subject to conditions. Steiglitz Reach to the entrance is a 6-knot zone.

The smaller and newer options

  • Hope Island Marina, on the Coomera River next to Sanctuary Cove: berths 12 to 30 m for monohulls and cats, about 40 minutes from the Seaway. Rentals from $1,050 a month for 12 m; freehold berths listed at $179,000 for 12 m and $320,000 for 18 m, with outgoings of $6,600 to $7,900 a year.
  • Coomera Waters Marina, Coomera River North Branch: 70 berths, 12 to 20 m, permanent and casual.
  • Sovereign Island Marina, Paradise Point: 30 berths, 10 to 45 m, digital gates and 24/7 CCTV; $10 million public liability cover required.
  • Mantaray Marina, The Spit: 67 berths from 10 to 60 m, twenty of them superyacht berths, bookings from April 2026 and officially open 1 August 2026. Monthly leases advertised from $2,000.
  • Main Beach Superyacht Marina, the old Mariner’s Cove: 66 berths to 73 m with high-flow fuel, a captain’s lounge and a helipad, alongside a Ritz-Carlton due 2029. The marina was targeted for the third quarter of 2026.

Getting up the river

The Seaway is 250 m wide with a permanent sand bypass, dredged to a minimum of about 4.5 m outside and 4.8 m inside. It is described as the safest bar in south-east Queensland, but on an ebb tide against a south-easterly swell it is not a place to be casual. Cross on the flood or at high slack, keep clear of the shallow bank north-east of the heads and log on with Seaway Tower or VMR Southport on VHF 16 or 73. Entrance dredging was under way in August 2026 and the sand-bypass jetty is closed to the public until the end of the year.

From the Broadwater to Coomera the numbers are simple: six knots, roughly 1.5 hours to GCCM, and about 3.0 m of water in the channel at Paradise Point, Sanctuary Cove and GCCM. Larger yachts go up on a rising tide and aim to arrive about an hour and a half before high water; the tide at Paradise Point runs about an hour behind the Seaway. Take the southern arm around Coomera Island. The channel from Sovereign Islands to the precinct is now on a regular dredging cycle, and the Coomera Connector bridge works have a temporary channel and reduced limits near the construction zone.

Worth knowing

Guideline limits for the run from the Seaway to the Coomera precinct without a pilot are 60 m and a 2.1 m draught; to Southport Yacht Club, 65 m and 3 m. Vessels over 50 m need a pilot unless exempt. Under-keel clearance is 1.0 m in the Seaway entrance and 0.5 m elsewhere.

Cyclone season

Tropical Cyclone Alfred brought gusts over 100 km/h to the Gold Coast in March 2025 and stripped about six million cubic metres of sand from the beaches. The Coomera River marinas came through it as the region’s storm shelter: The Boat Works hauled 275 boats and secured 125 in the water without a scratch, and Hope Harbour’s off-river basin did what it was built for. Most policies require you to haul out or secure the vessel within a set time once a cyclone is named, and yard space goes quickly, so the marina you choose is part of your insurance plan whether you think of it that way or not.

How to choose

  • You run out the Seaway most weekends: Main Beach, Runaway Bay or Mantaray. Pay for the position.
  • She is in the yard a lot, or you want the trades on hand: The Boat Works or GCCM. Everything is a golf-cart ride away, including us.
  • You want the resort and do not mind the river: Sanctuary Cove, with Hope Island and Hope Harbour as the quieter neighbours.
  • You want certainty of a berth for the long haul: consider buying at Hope Island or GCCM, and weigh the outgoings against rent.
  • Cyclones worry you: upriver, and a yard that can lift her.

Questions owners ask

Which Gold Coast marina is closest to open water?

Marina Mirage and Southport Yacht Club at Main Beach are about five minutes from the Seaway. Runaway Bay is two nautical miles away. Hope Harbour says 15 minutes. The Coomera precinct, The Boat Works and Gold Coast City Marina, is about 11 nautical miles upstream, roughly an hour and a half at the 6-knot river limit.

How much does a marina berth cost on the Gold Coast?

Only Southport Yacht Club publishes a full rate card. In May 2026 a 59 ft single berth was $2,064 a month for Gold Members and $3,145 for visitors, and a 66 ft berth was $2,598 and $3,380. Gold Coast City Marina’s last published schedule, from November 2022, had 61–70 ft berths at $1,925 a month. Mantaray at The Spit advertises leases from $2,000 a month. Everyone else quotes on enquiry.

Can I live aboard at a Gold Coast marina?

It varies. Southport Yacht Club publishes a live-aboard rate. The Boat Works has live-aboard ensuites and a hardstand area for up to 16 live-aboard boats. Runaway Bay allows a night or two but not full time. Pacific Point (Horizon Shores) permits it subject to conditions. Sanctuary Cove, GCCM, Hope Harbour and Marina Mirage do not publish a policy, so ask.

Is the Coomera River deep enough for a 60 or 70 ft motor yacht?

Yes, with tide awareness. The Coomera River channel is maintained at about 3.0 m at Paradise Point, Sanctuary Cove and GCCM, and The Boat Works quotes 3.5 m at low tide. Larger yachts time the passage for mid to high tide and arrive about an hour and a half before high water. Guideline limits for the precinct are 60 m and a 2.1 m draught without a pilot.

What is the speed limit on the Coomera River?

Six knots, effectively all the way. From Paradise Point and Sovereign Islands up both branches of the river, all marinas and canals, and the Hope Harbour entrance are gazetted at 6 knots for vessels of your size. Plan on about an hour and a half from the Seaway to Coomera.

Where do Gold Coast yachts go in a cyclone?

Up the river. During Tropical Cyclone Alfred in March 2025, The Boat Works secured about 400 vessels, 275 hauled out and 125 in the water, and reported no damage. Hope Harbour’s basin sits off the river and out of the tidal flow. Most insurance policies require you to haul out or secure within a set time once a cyclone is named, so book the yard before everyone else does.
