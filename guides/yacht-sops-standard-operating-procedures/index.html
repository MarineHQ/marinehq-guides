Yacht SOPs: what standard operating procedures are, why they pay for themselves, and what a good set includes

What an SOP is on a private motor yacht, why checklists work, which procedures every yacht should have written down, how they help with crew changes, insurance and resale, and how Marine HQ writes them and puts them on your phone.

Updated 4 October 2026·7 min read·Gold Coast, Queensland

An SOP, a Standard Operating Procedure, is the written way a job is done on your yacht. Not how a generator works in general, but how this generator is started on this boat, in what order, with which breakers, and what to check before you walk away. A commercial vessel has to have them. A private yacht does not, and most do not, which is why so much of what a yacht knows lives in one person’s head. Here is what they are, why they work, which ones matter, and how we write them.

Key facts

  • What it is — one or two pages: the steps in order, the limits, and a checklist, written for one yacht
  • Required? — for commercial vessels, yes, under Marine Order 504; for a private yacht, no
  • Why bother — the boat runs the same way whoever is aboard, and nothing depends on memory
  • Start with — pre-departure and stand-down, then anchoring, tender, fuelling, power changeover and emergencies
  • Where they live — in print aboard, and on your phone in the MyYacht app
  • Ready-made — the three everyday checklists are $7; anything specific to your yacht is written for her

Why a checklist works

The best evidence comes from surgery. In 2009 a World Health Organization study across eight hospitals introduced a 19-item checklist read aloud before, during and after an operation. Major complications fell from 11% to 7% of patients, and deaths from 1.5% to 0.8%. Nothing about the surgeons’ skill changed. What changed was that the routine steps stopped depending on memory, and that everyone in the room knew the plan.

Aviation has worked this way for decades, and the lesson transfers directly to a yacht. The people who run boats well are not the ones who remember everything. They are the ones who do not have to.

What goes wrong without them

  • The seacock left open, the shore power left off, the hatch left ajar. Most damage at the dock is something left on, left open or left unrecorded after a trip.
  • Two people, two methods. The owner anchors one way, the weekend skipper another, and the windlass pays for the difference.
  • The guest brief that never happens. Life jackets, the raft, the fire blanket, and which door not to open under way.
  • The captain who leaves. Yacht crew turnover runs at 20 to 40% a year, and a 2023 survey found 40% of captains planning to look for a new boat. A proper handover is meant to include the equipment lists, the procedures for critical equipment, the maintenance history and the open defects. If none of that is written down, there is nothing to hand over.

What the law says

A domestic commercial vessel in Australia must operate under a safety management system. Marine Order 504, revised from 1 June 2025, requires documented procedures for onboard operations and emergencies, crew training and drills, and clear roles and responsibilities. That is the standard a charter yacht works to.

A recreational yacht is outside that system. AMSA is specific that paying someone to operate or crew her does not make her commercial. So a private owner has no obligation to write anything down, which is exactly why the well-run private yachts borrow the commercial habit.

Worth knowing

If there is any chance your yacht will go into charter, the procedures are the first thing the safety management system needs. Writing them while she is private is far easier than writing them the month before survey.

What a good SOP looks like

  1. A header that says which boat and which equipment. Vessel, the system it covers, the date and who wrote it.
  2. The purpose in one line. What the procedure is for and when it is used.
  3. The limits. Wind, depth, load, engine state: the conditions under which you do not do this.
  4. The steps, in the order you do them. Short lines, one action each, using the names on her own switches and valves.
  5. The warnings where they apply. Next to the step, not in a paragraph at the end.
  6. A checklist. The same steps as tick boxes, for the day you are doing it rather than learning it.
  7. What to do when it does not work. The windlass will not retrieve; the generator will not take the load.

One or two pages. If it is longer, it is a manual, and it will stay in the drawer.

How SOPs help beyond the dock

Crew changes. A new captain or a relief skipper reads the procedures and runs her the way she has always been run. The owner does not spend a season re-teaching the boat.

Insurance. No Australian policy asks for SOPs by name. But they exclude claims when the boat is under the control of someone without adequate experience to control her, and the proposal form asks about your experience, any boating course, and how the boat is maintained. A yacht with written procedures and a schedule is an easier story to tell an underwriter, and an easier one to tell after an incident.

Resale. A buyer is paying for the boat and for the evidence that she was looked after. A bound set of procedures, the maintenance schedule and the records is what a surveyor asks to see.

Family and guests. The person most likely to be at the helm when you are not is someone who knows the boat less well than you do.

Questions owners ask

What is an SOP on a yacht?

A Standard Operating Procedure is the written, agreed way a job is done on a particular yacht: leaving the berth, shutting her down, launching the tender, starting the generator, taking fuel. It is short, specific to her equipment, and usually ends in a checklist. A manual tells you how a pump works; an SOP tells you how this boat is run.

Does a private yacht legally need SOPs?

No. In Australia a domestic commercial vessel must have a safety management system under Marine Order 504, with documented procedures for onboard operations and emergencies. A recreational yacht is not a commercial vessel, even with paid crew, so nothing compels it. The reason to have them is practical: they are how a boat keeps running the same way when the people change.

Which SOPs should every motor yacht have?

Pre-departure and stand-down, because they are used on every trip. After that: anchoring and anchor retrieval, tender launch and recovery, fuelling, shore power and generator changeover, and the emergency procedures for fire, flooding and a person overboard. Add one for anything on your yacht that is expensive to get wrong: stabilisers, a passerelle, a watermaker, a tender garage.

How long should an SOP be?

One or two pages. A header with the vessel and the equipment it covers, the steps in order, the limits and warnings that apply, and a checklist. If it runs to ten pages nobody will use it on the dock, which is the only place it matters.

Do SOPs help with insurance or resale?

They help with both, indirectly. Australian policies exclude claims when the boat is under the control of someone without adequate experience to control her, and insurers ask about your experience and how the boat is maintained. A yacht with written procedures, a maintenance schedule and records is an easier risk to explain. At sale, the same folder is what a surveyor and a buyer ask for.

Can I just buy a template?

For the routines that are the same on every boat, yes: our Owner Checklists cover pre-departure, stand-down and wash-down for $7. For anything specific to your yacht, a template is the wrong tool. The value of an SOP is that it names her switches, her valves and her limits.
